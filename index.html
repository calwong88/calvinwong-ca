Foreword

Every account I've ever touched belonged to someone else. That's the job — and it's also, I've come to realize, the whole discipline of security.

I didn't come to cybersecurity through a computer science degree or a childhood spent breaking into systems. I came to it through years of protecting the financial lives of BMO's private banking clients — reading documentation closely enough to catch what shouldn't be there, keeping records the way an auditor would want to find them, and treating every piece of client data as something I was trusted with, not something I owned. Somewhere in that work, I realized I already had the instincts the field asks for: least privilege, verification before trust, discretion as a default. The certifications and the labs came after, to give that instinct a name and a technical vocabulary.

This site is where that transition lives — the resume, the hands-on projects, and the reasoning behind the move. I'm not pivoting away from years of financial operations experience. I'm applying it somewhere it's been needed the whole time.

About Calvin

I'm a financial-services operations professional based in Toronto, currently working toward a career in cybersecurity. Over the past several years at BMO, I've built a career on discretion, precision, and reliability — reviewing confidential client documentation, supporting audit and risk-review readiness, and safeguarding sensitive information every day. I hold certifications in security fundamentals and identity and access management, and I'm most effective working with sustained focus on detail-critical, confidentiality-sensitive tasks.

I'm looking for an entry-level opportunity with a Canadian financial institution where accuracy, trustworthiness, and a security-first mindset make the difference.

Why Cybersecurity

Every role I've held in banking has come down to the same core responsibility: protecting information that isn't mine, exactly the way it's supposed to be protected. Reviewing client documentation, catching discrepancies before they become problems, keeping records audit-ready — that's security work, even when the job title says "operations." Certifications gave me the vocabulary and frameworks to match; my own IAM and cloud-security labs (see Projects) gave me the hands-on practice. Cybersecurity is the natural next step, not a pivot.
